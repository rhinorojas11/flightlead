# Business model

## Positioning

FlightLead is an enrollment-response system for independent flight schools, not a generic chatbot and not a replacement for scheduling/operations software. It helps a school convert existing inquiries by responding, qualifying, and routing them more consistently.

The initial offer is a done-for-you pilot. The founder configures the approved knowledge, messaging number, escalation destination, and reporting process for each customer. Repeated implementation patterns become standardized only after multiple customers prove they are common.

## Offer hypothesis

An initial package to test:

- approximately $1,000 setup;
- approximately $500 per month;
- inbound SMS response and FAQ handling;
- conversational qualification and lead scoring;
- staff alerts for high-intent leads; and
- a simple monthly outcome review assembled from database records.

These numbers are experiments, not validated market rates or revenue forecasts. Early discounts should buy something concrete: rapid feedback, reference access, or permission to publish anonymized results.

## Customer discovery

Interview owners, general managers, chief instructors, and admissions staff. Ask for actual recent examples rather than opinions:

- What happens to inquiries after hours?
- How long did the last ten web/text leads wait for a response?
- Where are leads recorded, and who owns follow-up?
- Which questions repeat before a discovery flight?
- Where do prospects most often disappear?
- How many discovery flights and enrollments came from last month's inquiries?
- What would staff need to see before trusting an automated first response?

Do not lead with an AI demo. Validate the response-time and follow-up problem first.

## Unit economics to capture

Track per customer: inbound conversations, outbound segments, model tokens/cost, workflow executions, support time, qualified leads, alerts, discovery-flight requests/bookings, enrollments, churn, and gross margin. Do not claim revenue attribution unless the customer agrees on the attribution rule.

## Path to product

1. **Service:** learn with one school and manual setup.
2. **Productized service:** operate the same core workflow for roughly three schools and standardize repeated work.
3. **SaaS:** add onboarding, accounts, dashboard, billing, and integrations only when demonstrated demand justifies them.

The control rule is simple: do not spend six months building a platform before a customer pays for the narrow outcome.
